# Claim allocation optimizer: evaluation report

Generated by `python -m bench.allocation_eval --households 500 --seed 20261011 --plans 200`; as-of date 2026-10-11; no language model involved.

The optimizer (`clauseguard/allocation.py`) decides which policy of a household pays what share of one bill under the
coordination rules of a rule pack (`clauseguard/rulepacks.py`): statutory schemes first, then employer group before individual,
then primary / pro-rata / excess, fixed benefits stacked. Within a layer of co-primary policies it searches the claiming order
exactly; every later payer is presented what is still unpaid. Baselines: **greedy** = claim in the order the policies are held;
**single** = the best single policy on the whole bill.

The PUF states no coordination-of-benefits clause. The loader tags every plan EXCESS, which makes any two plans circular; the households are therefore evaluated twice: as co-primary (claiming order searched) and as loaded (circular, flagged, resolved as a rateable proportion: charges split by limit, each plan applying its own deductible and coinsurance to its share). The rateable resolution leaves more unpaid than sequential claiming, which is why greedy exceeds it there: the pro-rata clause, not the optimizer, is what costs the member.

## 5. Reading the numbers

- The indemnity principle holds in every household: the indemnity steps never add up to more than the bill (asserted in `tests/test_allocation.py`).
- Greedy is suboptimal when the first policy held is not the one to claim first: a plan whose deductible or out-of-pocket maximum binds on the full bill leaves a remainder that the second plan pays better than the other way round. The gain is the difference in cost sharing, not extra money.
- Greedy can exceed the optimizer only by breaching a coordination rule (claiming an excess or individual policy ahead of the statutory or group payer); the count is reported per study.
- Fixed-benefit covers (hospital cash) stack and are identical in all three strategies; the comparison is driven by the indemnity layer.
